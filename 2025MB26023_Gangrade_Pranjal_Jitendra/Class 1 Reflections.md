# Reflection: Session 1

25 July 2026 | Gangrade Pranjal Jitendra | 2025MB26023

---

The class gave good definitions of a product manager. Bridging business and engineering. Managing the lifecycle. I would have given one of those and been pleased with it. Then the faculty pointed out that nobody had said ownership, and I recognised the gap as mine.

Eight years in Salesforce delivery has trained me to describe my job in bridging language. That framing has served me well and it is also a way of standing slightly outside the outcome. If a project goes badly, the bridge was fine and the traffic was the problem. I have never said that out loud but I have certainly thought in that shape. The cricket captain point is what made it uncomfortable. You cannot claim the captaincy only in the weeks you win.

**Where I disagree.** The faculty said "engineering failed to deliver" is not available to you because engineering belongs to you. In a services firm it genuinely does not. Staffing is decided by a resourcing function optimising bench utilisation across the practice. I have lost an architect mid build and had no vote in it. The honest version for my context is that the consequence is mine whether or not the control was. That is a less inspiring sentence and I think it is the true one. If I want the authority to match, I have to go and take it rather than wait for it.

**The story that landed.** An architect proposed a technically better but exotic database and the faculty declined it, because the talent pool was thin and he did not want the product or the person locked in. The decision turned on a factor absent from the technical scorecard.

I have made the opposite call. I let a team build a clever custom framework on standard functionality. It was better on the merits. It also meant every new joiner needed two weeks of ramp time on something unusable elsewhere, and when the original builder rolled off, velocity never recovered. I evaluated the design and not the staffing consequences of the design, because I did not think of that as my column to fill in. That may be the real difference between a technical lead and a product owner. Not depth, but which columns you treat as yours.

**What I will do.** On every design proposal, write one line on who maintains this in eighteen months. If I cannot answer it, that is information about the decision. Second, explain my rejections properly. I decline things often and explain myself inconsistently, usually depending on how busy I am.

**Open question.** My firm is adopting AI to build products and talking about AI inside products. Nobody is using AI for the thinking work of prioritisation. That gap is either an oversight or it says something about how much our decision work is valued.
