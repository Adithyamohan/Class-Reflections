**Personal Reflections Session 7**



* **Problem First:** The discussion around the agricultural advisory example made it clear that the starting point should be the farmer's actual problem rather than simply deciding to use AI. This changed how I think about identifying AI opportunities.
* **Customer Value:** The Netflix recommendation example showed that AI creates value when it helps users discover content more easily. The important part is not the recommendation model itself, but the improvement in the user's experience.
* **AI vs Simple Solutions:** The examples discussed in class showed that not every problem needs AI. A simple rule, automation or search-based solution can sometimes be more suitable, which is an important consideration for a PM.
* Testing Assumptions: The UPI dispute assistance example helped me understand how an AI product idea should be connected to a specific customer problem and then tested to see whether it actually improves the process.
* Customer Adoption: The discussion around customers moving from an existing product to a new one made me realise that even a better solution can face resistance. Switching effort, training and existing investments can have a major impact on adoption.
* AI Uncertainty: The examples involving AI recommendations made me think about what happens when the system is wrong. The PM needs to consider the consequences of incorrect outputs instead of looking only at successful cases.
* **Human Role: T**he class discussion showed that AI can support a person's decision without necessarily replacing them. This seems especially important in situations where the final decision has significant consequences.
* Risk of Failure: The discussion about tail risks was an important learning for me. A system may perform well most of the time, but a small number of serious failures can still make it unsuitable for a particular use case.
* Trust: The examples discussed around AI adoption showed that technical performance alone is not enough. Users need to understand and trust the system before they are comfortable depending on it.
* **Workflow Fit:** The discussion on existing workflows made me realise that AI should fit into the way people already work. Simply adding an AI step may not create value if it makes the overall process more complicated.
* **Measurement:** The class made it clear that an AI product cannot be judged only by model accuracy. The actual customer outcome, adoption, cost and impact on the workflow also need to be measured.
* **Product Responsibility:** The different examples made me understand that the PM has to think about the complete picture-customer value, feasibility, adoption, risks and outcomes-not just whether the AI model works.
* **Overall Learning:** The examples from agriculture, UPI and Netflix helped connect the concepts to real products. The main takeaway was that good AI product management is about finding the right problem, choosing the right level of AI and proving that it creates meaningful value.
