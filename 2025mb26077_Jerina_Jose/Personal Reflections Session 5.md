**Personal Reflections Session 5**



* The biggest change in my thinking is that AI should start with a customer problem, not with the technology. The first question should be where AI can actually add value.
* Looking at the customer's workflow makes it easier to understand where the real problem exists. The goal should be to help the customer complete their work more easily, quickly or effectively.
* The idea that customers pay for value and not simply for the word “AI” made me think differently about AI products. Technology alone is not enough to justify a product.
* A product idea should be treated as something that needs to be tested. Evidence from experiments is more useful than assuming that a solution will create value.
* Reducing repeated decisions or unnecessary effort can itself be a strong opportunity for AI. Not every AI product needs to create a completely new workflow.
* Data-rich and repetitive workflows can be good areas to explore for AI, but they still need to be evaluated rather than treated as automatic opportunities.
* Trust becomes more important when AI is involved. An AI system that gives uncertain or incorrect answers can reduce trust instead of improving it.
* Uncertainty should not be hidden from users. The product needs to show when confidence is low and provide a suitable next step.
* Human involvement should depend on the level of uncertainty and risk. Some situations can be handled by AI, while others may need review or escalation.
* Human-in-the-Loop: Human review should have a clear purpose. When a case is escalated, the reviewer should have enough information to make the final decision.
* The human-in-the-loop discussion made me realise that simply adding a human reviewer is not enough. The product should clearly explain why a case was escalated and provide the information needed for the person to make the decision.
* If people are measured only by the number of decisions or tasks completed, they may focus on quantity rather than actually solving the problem.
* The PM role appears more challenging than simply managing features and timelines. It involves understanding the customer's real work, deciding where AI makes sense, managing uncertainty and proving that the product is actually creating value.
