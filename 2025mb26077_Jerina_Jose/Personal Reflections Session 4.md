**Personal Reflections Session 4**



* **AI and Value:** The class changed the way I look at AI products. Having AI in a product is not enough; there should be a clear improvement in the value delivered to the customer.
* **Problem First:** Starting with the problem rather than the technology makes more sense. This can prevent building an AI solution simply because the technology is available.
* Starting with the problem and understanding the user's needs before selecting the technology makes the product decision more meaningful.
* Customer value should be considered from the user's point of view and not only from the product team's perspective.
* A simpler solution can sometimes solve a problem better than an AI solution, so using AI does not automatically make a product better.
* A product idea is a hypothesis that needs to be tested with evidence before making bigger decisions.
* AI can make mistakes, so understanding the impact of wrong answers and having ways to manage the risk is important.
* Measuring an AI product is not only about accuracy; cost, response time, user experience and actual business outcomes also matter.
* AI does not always need to replace people and can instead support them while important decisions remain with humans.
* The success of an AI product depends more on how it improves the user's workflow than on how advanced the AI model is.
* Trust is an important part of an AI product because users need confidence in the system before they are willing to use it.
* An AI product should fit naturally into the existing workflow without creating additional and unnecessary work.





* **Workflow**: A stable and well-understood workflow is easier to improve or automate. If the workflow itself is unclear or constantly changing, solving the underlying process may need to come first.
* **Uncertainty**: AI products involve uncertainty, so it is important to identify what is known, what is assumed and what still needs to be tested.
* **Human Judgment:** AI decision support should not make people stop thinking for themselves. Keeping human judgment involved is especially important when the consequences of a wrong decision are high.
* **Guardrails:** Good AI products need safeguards for situations where the system may fail or behave unexpectedly. These safeguards should be considered as part of the product design, not added at the end.
* **Social Legitimacy:** Before looking only at technical feasibility, the product also needs to be acceptable and trustworthy to the people who will use or be affected by it.
* **Data and Decisions:** The discussion on decision rights made me realise that someone needs to be responsible for deciding what data can be used, what knowledge can be included and which models are appropriate.
* **Measurement:** An AI product needs evidence that it is improving the intended outcome. Without measurement, it becomes difficult to know whether the AI is actually helping.
* **Overall Learning:** The class made me see an AI Product Manager as someone who has to balance customer value, technology, uncertainty, risk and adoption rather than simply deciding which AI model to use.
