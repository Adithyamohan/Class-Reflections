**Personal Reflections Session 3**



* **Product Manager Role**: A PM is not just someone who manages a product. A PM has to take ownership of the decisions, priorities, resources and outcomes.
* **Ownership and Responsibility**: A PM is a custodian of the trust, time and resources given to them. This made me look at product decisions with more responsibility.
* **Decision Traceability**: Keeping a clear connection between a decision and the evidence behind it. It helps explain why a decision was taken and what we learned from it later.
* Progress Over Features: Adding more features does not always mean the product is progressing. What matters more is whether the product is actually moving closer to solving the user's problem.
* Influence Without Authority: PMs cannot always depend on formal authority. They need to influence different teams through communication, reasoning and a clear understanding of the product goal.
* **Frameworks**: Frameworks are useful for helping us think through a problem, but they should not become something we follow blindly.
* **Jobs to Be Done**: This changed my thinking about users. Instead of immediately thinking about features or personas, I should first understand what the user is actually trying to accomplish.
* **Product Lifecycle**: Product lifecycle is more like a learning loop. Even after launch, the learning should take us back to discovery and help us make better decisions.
* **AI Product Management**: An AI product is not simply about choosing a good AI model. The PM has to decide where AI actually adds value and how its uncertainty and risks should be managed.
* Working With Uncertainty: PMs often have to make decisions without having complete information. The important thing is to use the available evidence, make a reasonable decision and learn from the outcome.
* **PM Artifacts**: Artifacts are not just documents created for the sake of the process. They help maintain clarity and connect the different decisions made throughout the product journey.
* **Experimentation**: Instead of assuming an idea will work, it is better to test important assumptions early and use the results to decide the next step.
* **Scrum and Kanban**: These methods are not only about tracking tasks. They help teams organise work, manage priorities and respond when requirements change.
* Product management is more about making responsible decisions than simply managing tasks. I now understand that a PM has to connect user needs, business value, evidence, team effort and learning while being accountable for the outcome.
