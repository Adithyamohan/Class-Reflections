**Personal Reflections Session 6**



* **Problem Before Technology:** The class changed my approach to AI product ideas. Starting with the customer's problem instead of starting with AI makes the product direction much clearer.
* **Customer Value:** A product should solve something important for the customer rather than simply having an attractive interface or advanced technology. The actual value comes from helping the customer get their job done.
* **Understanding Requirements:** Customer requirements should be understood in the customer's own language before converting them into technical requirements. This helps avoid making assumptions about what the customer really needs.
* **Using PM Judgment:** Not every request from a stakeholder needs to become a product feature. Understanding the request and using product judgment to decide what should actually be built is an important PM responsibility.
* **Right Customer Segment:** Trying to satisfy everyone can make a product complicated and less useful. Focusing on a clearly defined user group and giving them enough value through the MVP makes more sense.
* **MVP:** An MVP should contain enough functionality for the target user to get real value. Making the product too complicated can increase the effort required to use it and reduce adoption.
* Cost of Adoption: Even a useful product may not succeed if using it requires too many additional steps. The overall effort for the customer needs to be considered while designing the solution.
* **Questioning Assumptions:** The class made me think about how easily assumptions can become part of product decisions. Questioning those assumptions can sometimes reveal a better or simpler way of solving the problem.
* **Reimagining the Workflow:** Instead of simply replacing one existing step with AI, there may be opportunities to reduce, combine or completely rethink some steps in the workflow.
* **AI/ML Translation:** A business problem cannot directly become an ML problem. It needs to be translated into a clear AI/ML target that can actually be modelled and evaluated.
* **Evaluation**: Building a model is only one part of the work. Having a clear way to evaluate whether the model is actually solving the intended problem is equally important.
* Data Quality: Data availability alone does not mean that the data is useful. Missing data, gaps, bias and reliability can directly affect the quality of an AI solution.
* Wrong Target: One important learning was that a model can optimise something very well and still be solving the wrong problem. Getting the business question and ML target right is therefore critical.
* Experimentation: Decisions should ideally be supported by experiments and evidence rather than only by personal preference or assumptions. This makes product decisions more objective.
* **Products That Last:** A product should be designed to continue creating value beyond the person or team that originally built it. This made product documentation, evaluation and clear decision-making seem more important.
* **Overall Learning**: The class made product management feel less like feature management and more like a continuous process of understanding problems, making decisions, testing assumptions and learning from evidence.
