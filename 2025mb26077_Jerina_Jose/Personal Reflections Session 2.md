**Personal Reflections Session 2**



* **Product Lifecycle**: The product lifecycle is not a straight journey from building to launching. Each stage creates learning that should take us back to discovery and help improve the next decision. Discover -> Define -> Strategize -> Plan -> Build -> Launch -> Grow -> Retire
* **PM Artifact System**: PM artifacts are not just documents created for process compliance; they help connect the problem, strategy, delivery, launch, and learning behind a product.
* **Tracing One Decision Across Artifacts**: One product decision can appear across different artifacts, and tracing it helps understand why the decision was made and whether it was successful.
* **Opportunity Trees \& Decision Mapping**: How a broad product goal can be broken into smaller opportunities and decisions instead of trying to solve everything at once.
* **Lean Canvas, Business Model Canvas \& PMF**: Good product idea is not enough; the product also needs a clear customer problem, business value, and evidence that the market actually wants it.
* **PESTEL \& SWOT**: Product decisions should consider both the external environment and the internal strengths and weaknesses of the business.
* **QFD \& House of Quality**: How customer expectations can be translated into specific product requirements and priorities.
* Basic PM principles can be reused across different products, but the way they are applied has to change depending on the users, market, industry, and product.
* Copying a framework exactly is not always effective. A PM needs to understand the purpose of the framework and adapt it to the actual situation.
* **Decision Rights and Uncertainty**: PM cannot make every decision alone, especially when there is uncertainty. Knowing who has the right expertise to make or validate a decision is just as important as making the decision itself.
* **Choosing the AI Product Strategy - Not Merely a Model**: Building an AI product is not simply about selecting the best AI model. The PM has to decide how AI will create user and business value and how it will fit into the overall product experience.
* AI products can involve different types of systems, such as prediction, recommendation, classification, generative AI, or decision-support systems. The PM’s responsibilities can change depending on the type of AI being used.
* AI-native products need more continuous validation because model performance, data quality, user feedback, and uncertainty can change over time. The product lifecycle therefore involves more learning and iteration.

**5 Product Manager Job Responsibilities \& My Analysis**



1. **Understand customer problems** - A PM identifies what users actually need rather than immediately proposing features. My reflection: I realised that understanding the problem properly can prevent a lot of unnecessary product work.
2. **Define product strategy and priorities** - A PM decides what should be focused on and what can wait. My reflection: This showed me that saying no to some ideas is also an important part of product management.
3. **Work with cross-functional teams** - PMs coordinate with engineering, design, business, operations, and other stakeholders. My reflection: I understood that communication and alignment are as important as technical knowledge.
4. **Make decisions under uncertainty** - PMs often have to make decisions without having complete information. My reflection: I learned that a PM does not always need perfect information; they need enough evidence to make a reasonable decision and learn from the result.
5. **Measure outcomes and improve the product** - A PM tracks whether the product is actually delivering value and uses feedback to decide what to change next. My reflection: This made me understand that a PM's responsibility does not end when the product is launched; accountability continues through learning and improvement.
