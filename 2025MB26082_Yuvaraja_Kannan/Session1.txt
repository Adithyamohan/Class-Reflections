Session 1 Reflection – Management of AI Products

This session kind of changed how I look at what a Product Manager actually does. Before this, I thought a PM was mainly there to collect requirements 
and act as a bridge between business folks and engineering. But when the professor called a PM the CEO of the product that hit different. 
It's not just about coordinating anymore  it's about actually owning the thing.

That made me think about my own job. My title says automation, but I'm constantly working with HR people, RPA developers, architects, business partners...
and a lot of the time I'm making calls even though the dev team doesn't report to me. Honestly I've probably been doing PM-type work for a while without 
ever calling it that.

The bit about accountability also stuck with me. The professor said a PM owns both the wins and the failures of the product. My first reaction was —
that's kind of unfair, since so much is out of your control. But thinking about it more, I get it now. Owning something doesn't mean doing every 
task yourself, it means making sure the product actually gets where it needs to go. That mindset feels really important if I actually want to move into 
AI Product Management down the line.

I also liked the point about PM being a thankless job. I've seen this exact thing happen on my automation projects — people are happy with a solution at first, 
then a few weeks later they just want more, without really acknowledging what already went into building it. The professor's take — that you should 
track your own progress instead of waiting for people to appreciate you —was such a simple but useful reframe. Basically, building something good 
should be enough of a reason on its own.

Takeaway for myself:
A PM isn't just someone who coordinates work , a PM owns the problem, the decisions, and the outcome.
